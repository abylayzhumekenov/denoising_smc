\section{Setup}

\subsection{Setting}

We consider the Bayesian inverse problem of drawing samples from the posterior
\begin{equation}\label{eq:posterior}
  p(x_0 \mid y) \propto p(y \mid x_0)\,p(x_0),
\end{equation}
where $x_0 \in \R^d$ and $y$ denotes observations.
The prior $p(x_0)$ is represented by a pretrained diffusion model.
The likelihood $p(y \mid x_0)$ is assumed known.

\subsection{Reverse diffusion and path measures}

A diffusion model provides a time-reversed process that, when integrated from a pure-noise
initialisation to zero noise, generates samples $x_0 \sim p(x_0)$.
In continuous time this process is an It\^o SDE
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sde-unguided}
  dx_\tau = a(x_\tau, \tau)\,d\tau + g(\tau)\,dW^{\bbP}_\tau,
\end{equation}
where $\tau$ is a reverse-time parameter and $a$, $g$ are drift and diffusion
coefficients of the reverse process.
For simplicity we take $g(\tau)$ to be scalar (diffusion coefficient $g(\tau)I$),
and $W^{\bbP}$ is a Brownian motion under the measure $\bbP$.
The path law of this process is denoted by $\bbP$.

To condition on $y$ we require the intermediate likelihood
$p(y \mid x_\tau) = \int p(y \mid x_0)\,p(x_0 \mid x_\tau)\,dx_0$, which is intractable.
We work with a generic approximation $\tilde p(y \mid x_\tau)$ of this intermediate likelihood;
its construction depends on the application and is deferred to the relevant sections.
We assume $\tilde p(y \mid x_\tau)$ is positive and differentiable in $x_\tau$.
Define the guidance drift
\begin{equation}\label{eq:guidance-drift}
  b(x_\tau, \tau) \coloneqq \nabla_{x_\tau}\!\log \tilde p(y \mid x_\tau).
\end{equation}
The guided reverse process augments the drift by a term proportional to $b$,
retaining the same diffusion coefficient $g$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sde-guided}
  dx_\tau = \bigl[\,a(x_\tau, \tau) + g(\tau)^2\,b(x_\tau, \tau)\bigr]\,d\tau
            + g(\tau)\,dW^{\bbQ}_\tau,
\end{equation}
with path law $\bbQ$.
The two processes share the same $g$; they differ \emph{only} in their drift.
This is the precise setting for Girsanov's theorem.

\subsection{Girsanov correction}

Let $h \coloneqq g^2 b$ be the drift difference.
Girsanov's theorem \citep[Theorem~8.6.6]{ksendal} states that $\bbP$ and $\bbQ$ are equivalent
and the Radon-Nikodym derivative is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:girsanov-continuous}
  \log\frac{d\bbP}{d\bbQ}
    = -\int \frac{h}{g}\cdot dW^{\bbQ}
      - \frac12\int \Bigl\|\frac{h}{g}\Bigr\|^2 d\tau.
\end{equation}
Substituting $h = g^2 b$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:girsanov-simplified}
  \log\frac{d\bbP}{d\bbQ}
    = -\int g\,b\cdot dW^{\bbQ}
      - \frac12\int g^2\,\|b\|^2\,d\tau.
\end{equation}
This is a continuous-time path integral.
We assume the usual regularity conditions for Girsanov's theorem
(progressive measurability of $g b$ and a Novikov-type integrability condition).
The particle filter operates in discrete time, so we partition the reverse trajectory
into $K$ steps.
Fix a decreasing noise schedule $\sigma_K > \sigma_{K-1} > \cdots > \sigma_0 \approx 0$,
indexed in reverse by $k = K,\dots,1$.
The reverse-time parameter $\tau$ is oriented so that integration over the step
runs from $\tau_k$ to $\tau_{k-1}$ (a positive increment).
Over step $k$ the accumulated squared diffusion is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta}
  \int_{\tau_k}^{\tau_{k-1}} g(\tau)^2\,d\tau = \sigma_k^{2} - \sigma_{k-1}^{2}
    \eqqcolon \delta_k \;>\; 0.
\end{equation}
This identification holds for variance-exploding additive-noise parametrisations
and is assumed throughout.
The realised Brownian increment under $\bbQ$ is
$\int_{\tau_k}^{\tau_{k-1}} g(\tau)\,dW^{\bbQ}_\tau = \sqrt{\delta_k}\; z_k$,
where $z_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ is the noise drawn during the proposal step.

The per-step contribution to the Radon-Nikodym derivative is the restriction of the
integrals in~\eqref{eq:girsanov-simplified} to $[\tau_k, \tau_{k-1}]$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:step-integral}
  \Delta_k \;\coloneqq\; -\int_{\tau_k}^{\tau_{k-1}} g\,b\cdot dW^{\bbQ}
                        - \frac12\int_{\tau_k}^{\tau_{k-1}} g^2\,\|b\|^2\,d\tau.
\end{equation}
The function $b(\tau)$ is known only at discrete points along the trajectory.
The standard It\^o discretisation of~\eqref{eq:step-integral} evaluates the
integrand at the left endpoint $x_k$, which is adapted to the Brownian filtration:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:girsanov}
  C_k \;\coloneqq\; -\sqrt{\delta_k}\; b_k^\top z_k
                 - \tfrac12\,\delta_k\,\|b_k\|^2,
  \qquad b_k \coloneqq b(x_k, \tau_k).
\end{equation}
More accurate Girsanov discretisations are possible but require proper stochastic
quadrature rather than a direct substitution of a different evaluation point.
Examples include subdiscretising the interval with adapted Brownian increments,
It\^o-Taylor expansions, midpoint or trapezoidal rules with Stratonovich-type
correction terms, and approximate discrete kernel ratios for specific proposals.
Optionally, the predictor-point evaluation $b_{\text{pred}}$ can be tested
empirically as a heuristic.
These refinements are catalogued in Appendix~\ref{app:alternatives}.
The left-endpoint rule is the simplest It\^o-consistent discretisation and coincides
exactly with the Gaussian kernel ratio when both $p(\cdot\mid x_k)$ and
$q(\cdot\mid x_k)$ are Euler-Maruyama kernels
(Appendix~\ref{app:girsanov-euler}).

\subsection{Sequential Monte Carlo}

The target measure over complete paths is $\nu_{0:K} \propto \tilde p(y \mid x_0)\,\bbP$.
We use the guided path law $\bbQ$ as the proposal.
The path-level importance weight is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:path-weight}
  w(x_{0:K}) \;\propto\; \tilde p(y \mid x_0)\;
                           \frac{d\bbP}{d\bbQ}(x_{0:K}).
\end{equation}

To obtain a sequential update, we factor $\tilde p(y \mid x_0)$ by telescoping
over the schedule:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:telescope}
  \tilde p(y \mid x_0)
    = \tilde p(y \mid x_K)
      \prod_{k=K}^{1} \frac{\tilde p(y \mid x_{k-1})}{\tilde p(y \mid x_k)}.
\end{equation}
(Products are taken over $k = K, K-1, \dots, 1$.)
Together with the factorisation of the Girsanov correction, this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weight-product}
  w \;\propto\; \tilde p(y \mid x_K)
                 \prod_{k=K}^{1}
                   \frac{\tilde p(y \mid x_{k-1})}{\tilde p(y \mid x_k)}\,
                   \exp(C_k).
\end{equation}
Define the initial weight $w_K \propto \tilde p(y \mid x_K)$.
Then at each step the incremental log weight is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unified-weight-raw}
  \log w_{k-1}
    = \log w_k
      + \bigl[\log\tilde p(y \mid x_{k-1}) - \log\tilde p(y \mid x_k)\bigr]
      + C_k.
\end{equation}
Resampling is triggered whenever the effective sample size drops below a threshold.

Equation~\eqref{eq:unified-weight-raw} uses the exact path-level Girsanov correction.
It is also useful to introduce two tunable parameters,
allowing a continuous interpolation between weighting strategies:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unified-weight}
  \log w_{k-1}
    = \log w_k
      + \rho\Bigl[\log\tilde p(y \mid x_{k-1}) - \log\tilde p(y \mid x_k)\Bigr]
      + \lambda\,C_k,
\end{equation}
where $\lambda \in \{0,1\}$ switches the Girsanov correction on ($1$) or off ($0$),
and $\rho > 0$ tempers the likelihood contribution.
When $\rho \neq 1$, the initial weight is $w_K \propto \tilde p(y \mid x_K)^\rho$
to preserve the telescoping identity.
The choice $\lambda = 1$, $\rho = 1$ yields the approximate posterior
$\tilde p(x_0\mid y) \propto \tilde p(y\mid x_0)\,p(x_0)$, up to discretisation
and Girsanov-quadrature error.
When $\tilde p(y \mid x_0) = p(y \mid x_0)$ this coincides with the exact
posterior~\eqref{eq:posterior}.
The choice $\lambda = 0$ drops the path-measure correction and recovers the
pseudo-bootstrap scheme of~\citet{millard2026particle}, which is generally biased
relative to the approximate posterior target.

\subsection{Proposals}\label{sec:proposals}

A particle filter requires a proposal kernel $q(x_{k-1}\mid x_k)$ that propagates
particles (the dependence on $y$ through the guidance drift is understood).
The proposal is obtained by discretising~\eqref{eq:sde-guided} with a numerical
integrator; the integrator determines both the transition $q$ and the trajectory
accuracy.
The Girsanov correction uses the left-endpoint rule, the baseline quadrature
applicable to any integrator.
More accurate integrator-specific corrections are catalogued in
Appendix~\ref{app:alternatives}.
We evaluate two integrators:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Guided Euler-Maruyama}~(GEM).  A first-order integrator
    producing a Gaussian proposal kernel (trajectory error $\mathcal{O}(\delta_k)$).
    The left-endpoint correction coincides exactly with the closed-form kernel ratio
    $\log\,p(x_{k-1}\!\mid\!x_k)/q(x_{k-1}\!\mid\!x_k)$
    (Appendix~\ref{app:girsanov-euler}).
    With $\lambda = 1$ the weight is therefore identical to the Twisted Diffusion
    Sampler~\citep{wu2023tds}.
    This arm serves as a verification baseline.
    One score evaluation per step.
  \item \textbf{Stochastic Heun}~(Heun-SDE).  A Runge-Kutta integrator
    for additive-noise SDEs, second-order in the drift
    (trajectory error $\mathcal{O}(\delta_k^2)$).
    The first stage predicts via Euler; the second stage corrects by averaging
    the drift at $x_k$ and the predicted midpoint $x_{\text{pred}}$;
    both stages are driven by the same noise $z_k$.
    The transition is non-Gaussian, so the exact finite-step kernel ratio is
    intractable; the left-endpoint correction provides an asymptotically valid
    weight, converging to the exact path-measure correction as $K \to \infty$.
    Replacing $b_k$ with $b_{\text{pred}}$ in the correction is a natural
    heuristic but not It\^o-consistent without Stratonovich-type corrections
    (Appendix~\ref{app:alternatives}).
    Two score evaluations per step.
\end{itemize}
The particle filter inherits two discretisation errors: trajectory error from the
integrator and weight error from the Girsanov quadrature.
With the left-endpoint rule the weight accuracy is first-order for both proposals;
a second-order integrator improves the trajectory while the weight remains at
the same order.
In practice this means both integrators produce comparably reliable weights,
while Heun-SDE yields a more accurate particle population.
For higher-order proposals the left-endpoint correction is safe but
potentially suboptimal: it does not exploit predictor information, and the
weight may become the limiting error.
Matched higher-order corrections are possible
(Appendix~\ref{app:alternatives}) but lie outside the current experiments.
Given $b_k$, evaluating $C_k$ adds only $\mathcal{O}(d)$ operations per particle per
step, so the computational overhead is negligible compared to the score evaluations.

\subsection{Outline}

The remainder of the note validates this weighting machinery.
Section~\ref{sec:toy} and~\ref{sec:pde} describe two application settings
with their respective notation.
Appendices~\ref{app:girsanov-euler} and~\ref{app:alternatives} collect the
Euler kernel-ratio verification and the catalogue of alternative Girsanov

\section{Toy model: Gaussian mixture}\label{sec:toy}

We validate the weighting machinery on a one-dimensional Gaussian-mixture model whose
every ingredient is available in closed form: the exact score, the exact intermediate
likelihood, and the analytic posterior. Any deviation of the SMC output from the analytic
posterior is therefore algorithm error, not model error. All quantities in this section
are computed in closed form from the mixture; no learned model is involved.

\subsection{Model}

The prior is a two-component mixture
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixture-prior}
  p_0(x) \;=\; \sum_{m=1}^{2} w_m\,\mathcal{N}\bigl(x;\; \mu_m,\; v_m\bigr),
\end{equation}
with $w=(0.6,0.4)$, $\mu=(-1.5,2.0)$, $v=(0.7,1.3)$.
The observation model is linear-Gaussian,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:obs-model}
  y \;=\; x_0 + \varepsilon,\qquad \varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\gamma^2),
\end{equation}
with $y=0.8$ and $\gamma^2=0.5$.
Prior and likelihood are conjugate, so the posterior $p(x_0\mid y)$ is again a
two-component mixture obtained by a per-component Kalman update:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:analytic-posterior}
  p(x_0\mid y)
  \;\propto\; \sum_{m=1}^{2}
    w_m\,\mathcal{N}\bigl(y;\; \mu_m,\; v_m+\gamma^2\bigr)\,
    \mathcal{N}\bigl(x_0;\; m_m^y,\; c_m^y\bigr),
\end{equation}
with component mean $m_m^y = \mu_m + \frac{v_m}{v_m+\gamma^2}(y-\mu_m)$ and variance
$c_m^y = \frac{v_m\,\gamma^2}{v_m+\gamma^2}$.
For the instance above the analytic posterior has mean $0.833$ and standard deviation
$0.802$.

\subsection{Noising and exact score}

We use the variance-exploding parametrisation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ve-forward}
  x_\sigma \;=\; x_0 + \sigma\,z,\qquad z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1),
\end{equation}
so that $\sigma^2$ is the accumulated noise variance and the identification
$\int_{\tau_k}^{\tau_{k-1}}g^2\,d\tau=\sigma_k^2-\sigma_{k-1}^2=\delta_k$ of
\eqref{eq:delta} is exact.
Because Gaussians are closed under convolution, the noised marginal is the same mixture
with widened variances $v_m+\sigma^2$, and the score is the responsibility-weighted
average
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-score}
  \nabla_x\log p_\sigma(x)
  \;=\; \sum_{m=1}^{2} \pi_m(x;\sigma)\,
       \frac{\mu_m - x}{v_m+\sigma^2},
  \qquad
  \pi_m(x;\sigma) \;=\;
   \frac{w_m\,\mathcal{N}\bigl(x;\;\mu_m,\;v_m+\sigma^2\bigr)}
        {\sum_{m'} w_{m'}\,\mathcal{N}\bigl(x;\;\mu_{m'},\;v_{m'}+\sigma^2\bigr)}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Intermediate likelihood and guidance}

The intermediate likelihood $p(y\mid x_\sigma)=\int p(y\mid x_0)\,p(x_0\mid x_\sigma)\,dx_0$
is known in closed form via the mixture-in-$y$ identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-likelihood}
  p(y\mid x_\sigma)
  \;=\; \sum_{m=1}^{2} \pi_m(x;\sigma)\,
       \mathcal{N}\bigl(y;\; m_m(x),\; \gamma^2+c_m(x)\bigr),
\end{equation}
with $m_m(x)=\mu_m+\frac{v_m}{v_m+\sigma^2}(x-\mu_m)$ and
$c_m(x)=\frac{v_m\sigma^2}{v_m+\sigma^2}$.
Guidance follows \eqref{eq:guidance-drift} with the exact drift
\begin{equation}\label{eq:guidance-exact}
  b(x_\sigma,\tau) \;=\; \nabla_{x_\sigma}\log p(y\mid x_\sigma),
\end{equation}
which is computed exactly by differentiating \eqref{eq:exact-likelihood}.
Because the responsibilities $\pi_m(x;\sigma)$ are a genuinely nonlinear function of $x$,
the Hessian $H_k=\nabla^2_{x_k}\log p(y\mid x_k)$ and the divergence $\nabla\!\cdot\! b$
are non-constant, so the alternative Girsanov discretisations of
Appendix~\ref{app:alternatives} are meaningfully distinguishable.
(A single-Gaussian prior would make $\log p(y\mid x_\sigma)$ quadratic in $x$ and
collapse all of them.)

\subsection{Schedule, weights, and the boundary term}

The reverse trajectory uses the EDM power-law schedule \citep{karras2022edm} with
$\rho=7$, $\sigma_{\max}=6$, $\sigma_{\min}=10^{-3}$, and $K$ steps, giving step
increments $\delta_k=\sigma_k^2-\sigma_{k-1}^2$ and realised Brownian increments
$\sqrt{\delta_k}\,z_k$ as in \eqref{eq:girsanov}.
The particle filter applies the unified weight \eqref{eq:unified-weight}, with adaptive
resampling whenever the effective sample size drops below half the particle count
\citep{chopin2020smc}.
Particles are initialised from the exact noised marginal $p_{\sigma_{\max}}$
(not white noise), so the initial weight is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundary-weight}
  w_K \;\propto\; \tilde p(y\mid x_K)^\rho,
\end{equation}
the boundary term required by the telescoping identity \eqref{eq:unified-weight-raw} once
$\rho\neq 1$; it is easily omitted in an implementation and must be kept when $\rho$ is
varied.

\subsection{Metrics}

In one dimension the Wasserstein-1 distance between the particle cloud and the analytic
posterior admits a closed form,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:W1}
  W_1\bigl(\hat p,\; p(\cdot\mid y)\bigr)
  \;=\; \int_{\R} \bigl| \hat F(x) - F(x) \bigr|\,dx,
\end{equation}
where $\hat F$ is the empirical CDF of the particles and $F$ is the analytic posterior
CDF obtained from \eqref{eq:analytic-posterior}.
Alongside $W_1$ we report the errors of the weighted posterior mean and standard
deviation, $|\hat m-m^\ast|$ and $|\hat s-s^\ast|$, the final effective sample size, and
the number of resampling events.
To attribute weight variance to its sources we also track the per-step decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weight-variance}
  \mathrm{Var}\bigl[\Delta\log w_k\bigr]
  \;=\; \rho^2\,\mathrm{Var}[\Delta\ell]
        + \lambda^2\,\mathrm{Var}[C_k]
        + 2\lambda\rho\,\mathrm{Cov}[\Delta\ell, C_k],
\end{equation}
with $\Delta\ell=\log\tilde p(y\mid x_{k-1})-\log\tilde p(y\mid x_k)$.

\subsection{Experiment: K-sweep and ESS stability}\label{sec:toy-k-sweep}

The immediate validation is a sweep of the number of reverse steps
$K\in\{500,1000,2000,4000,8000\}$ at fixed $\sigma_{\max}$ and $\sigma_{\min}$,
$N=512$ particles, and $(\lambda,\rho)=(1,1)$, over both proposals of
Section~\ref{sec:proposals} and the Girsanov variants of
Appendix~\ref{app:alternatives} that are tractable in one dimension: the left-endpoint
baseline \eqref{eq:girsanov}, the subdiscretised \eqref{eq:subdisc}, the It\^o-Taylor
\eqref{eq:ito-taylor}, the trapezoidal and hybrid rules, and the predictor-point
heuristic.
All of them are exact or cheap in one dimension: $H_k$ and $\nabla\!\cdot\!b$ reduce to
scalar derivatives of \eqref{eq:exact-likelihood}, subdiscretisation costs a few extra
score evaluations, and the discrete kernel ratio of Appendix~\ref{app:alternatives} can
be evaluated numerically for the Heun-SDE proposal as an optional reference arm.

\textbf{Primary question.}
Does the effective sample size stay near $N$ as $K\to\infty$, or does some method
collapse?
Our analysis predicts stability for every arm: the leading term
$-\sqrt{\delta_k}\,b_k^\top z_k$ of each incremental log-weight is zero-mean with
variance of order $\delta_k$, so the total weight variance is a Riemann sum converging to
a constant as $K\to\infty$; the higher-order corrections \eqref{eq:subdisc} and
\eqref{eq:ito-taylor} add only $O(\delta_k^2)$ variance and cannot change this.
For the pseudo-bootstrap weight ($\lambda=0$) the claim holds exactly before any limit is
taken: the telescoping likelihood-ratio sum collapses to a single endpoint difference
$\rho[\log\tilde p(y\mid x_0)-\log\tilde p(y\mid x_K)]$, a bounded random variable whose
variance is independent of $K$.
Neither mechanism predicts ESS collapse in $K$ alone; the sweep verifies this empirically
and flags any variant whose discretisation error fails to decrease fast enough to
maintain it.

\textbf{Secondary question.}
Consistent $\lambda=1$ arms should converge to the analytic posterior
\eqref{eq:analytic-posterior} as $K\to\infty$ (both trajectory and weight error vanish),
while any variant carrying a persistent quadrature bias should plateau at a finite $W_1$
floor.
The predictor-point heuristic is the natural candidate for a persistent bias because it
is not It\^o-consistent without extra correction terms
(Appendix~\ref{app:alternatives}).
For the left-endpoint baseline on GEM the weight is exact at every $\delta_k$
(Appendix~\ref{app:girsanov-euler}), so $W_1$ should be flat in $K$ within Monte Carlo
noise while the ESS remains near $N$; Heun-SDE's approximate weight is unbiased to within
the noise floor at this particle count.
Any deviation from this pattern isolates a discretisation or implementation error.

\subsection{Experiment: $\lambda$ sweep}

Before the full K-sweep, a complementary sweep fixes $K$ and varies
$\lambda\in\{0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1\}$ at $\rho=1$.
This directly validates the unified weight~\eqref{eq:unified-weight}:
the Girsanov correction strength $\lambda$ should interpolate continuously
between the pseudo-bootstrap target ($\lambda=0$) and the exact posterior target
($\lambda=1$).  In particular the weighted posterior variance error $|\hat s-s^\ast|$
is predicted to decline monotonically with $\lambda$, and the effective sample
size should remain near $N$ for all $\lambda$ since the dominant stochastic
component cancels in the sum $\rho\Delta\ell+\lambda C_k$.

\subsection{Deferred experiments}

The following experiments are planned but run one at a time, after the K-sweep of
Section~\ref{sec:toy-k-sweep}:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{$\lambda$-$\rho$ grid.} $\lambda\in\{0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1\}$ and
        $\rho\in\{0.5,1,2\}$, with the boundary initialisation \eqref{eq:boundary-weight}.
        This interpolates between the exact target $(\lambda{=}1,\rho{=}1)$, the tempered
        pseudo-bootstrap ($\lambda{=}0$), and fractional corrections, and confirms that
        $\rho\neq 1$ targets $\tilde p(y\mid x_0)^\rho\,p(x_0)$.
        (The $\lambda$ sweep at $\rho=1$ has been completed; the $\rho$ sweep is deferred.)
  \item \textbf{Sharp-observation stress.} $\gamma^2\in\{0.5,0.1,0.02\}$.
        Smaller $\gamma$ sharpens $\tilde p$ and amplifies $\|b\|$, increasing the
        incremental weight variance; this is a more likely axis of ESS collapse than $K$
        itself.
  \item \textbf{Convergence order.} Log-log study of
        $|C_k^{\text{variant}}-C_k^{\text{ref}}|$ versus $\delta$ to confirm the advertised
        orders (left-endpoint $O(\delta)$, trapezoidal and hybrid $O(\delta^2)$).
  \item \textbf{Numerical kernel ratio.} For the non-Gaussian Heun-SDE proposal, evaluate
        the discrete kernel ratio of Appendix~\ref{app:alternatives} numerically as a
        reference weight.
\end{itemize}

\section{PDE-constrained reconstruction}\label{sec:pde}

The same machinery transfers to PDE-constrained reconstruction (Burgers and Darcy
solvers), where the prior is a pretrained diffusion model over solution fields and the
likelihood combines sparse sensor observations with a PDE residual.
The exact intermediate likelihood of the toy, \eqref{eq:exact-likelihood}, is intractable
in this setting; the guided samplers replace it with a surrogate formed from scaled
observation and residual losses, whose gradient plays the role of the guidance drift
\eqref{eq:guidance-exact}.
The weighting machinery of Section~\ref{sec:toy-k-sweep} transfers directly.
Details of scaling, sensor masks, and the experimental design are deferred; the toy model
above serves as the closed-form ground truth for the weighting machinery before any
trained network is involved.

\section{Euler-Maruyama kernel ratio}\label{app:girsanov-euler}

For the Euler-Maruyama discretisation, both the unguided kernel
$p(x_{k-1}\mid x_k)$ and the guided proposal $q(x_{k-1}\mid x_k)$ are Gaussian
with common covariance $\delta_k I$, differing only in their means:
\begin{align*}
  p(x_{k-1}\mid x_k) &= \mathcal{N}\bigl(x_{k-1};\; \mu_p,\; \delta_k I\bigr),\\
  \mu_p &\coloneqq x_k + \delta_k\,\nabla_{x_k}\!\log p(x_k),\\[2pt]
  q(x_{k-1}\mid x_k) &= \mathcal{N}\bigl(x_{k-1};\; \mu_q,\; \delta_k I\bigr),\\
  \mu_q &\coloneqq x_k + \delta_k\bigl(\nabla_{x_k}\!\log p(x_k) + b_k\bigr)
          = \mu_p + \delta_k b_k.
\end{align*}
Drawing $x_{k-1}$ from $q$ gives $x_{k-1} = \mu_q + \sqrt{\delta_k}\,z_k$ with
$z_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)$.
The log density ratio is therefore
\begin{align*}
  \log\frac{p(x_{k-1}\mid x_k)}{q(x_{k-1}\mid x_k)}
  &= -\frac{1}{2\delta_k}\Bigl(
       \|x_{k-1}-\mu_p\|^2 - \|x_{k-1}-\mu_q\|^2\Bigr)\\[2pt]
  &= -\frac{1}{2\delta_k}\Bigl(
       \|\sqrt{\delta_k}z_k + \delta_k b_k\|^2 - \|\sqrt{\delta_k}z_k\|^2\Bigr)\\[2pt]
  &= -\sqrt{\delta_k}\;b_k^\top z_k - \tfrac12\delta_k\,\|b_k\|^2
     \;=\; C_k.
\end{align*}
Thus, for the Euler-Maruyama discretisation, the Girsanov correction $C_k$
equals the exact Gaussian kernel ratio identically at every step size $\delta_k$.

\section{Alternative Girsanov discretisations}\label{app:alternatives}

The left-endpoint rule~\eqref{eq:girsanov} is the simplest It\^o-consistent
single-step discretisation of the Girsanov integral.
This appendix catalogues several refinements that, though not used in the
current experiments, remain practically testable.

\subsection{Subdiscretised Girsanov correction}

Split step~$k$ into $m$ substeps with subincrements
$\delta_{k,1},\dots,\delta_{k,m}$ such that $\sum_{j=1}^{m}\delta_{k,j}=\delta_k$.
Draw adapted Brownian subincrements $\Delta B_{k,j}=\sqrt{\delta_{k,j}}\,z_{k,j}$
and evaluate $b$ before each subincrement is drawn.
The per-step correction becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subdisc}
  C_k^{\text{sub}}
  =
  -\sum_{j=1}^{m} \sqrt{\delta_{k,j}}\; b(x_{k,j})^\top z_{k,j}
  -\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{m} \delta_{k,j}\,\|b(x_{k,j})\|^2.
\end{equation}
This is It\^o-adapted and converges to the continuous Girsanov correction as
the submesh is refined.
Cost: $m$ additional evaluations of $b$ per step.

\subsection{It\^o-Taylor correction}

Let $H_k \coloneqq \nabla^2_{x_k}\log\tilde p(y\mid x_k)$ be the Hessian of
$\log\tilde p$.
A first-order It\^o-Taylor expansion of the stochastic integral yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ito-taylor}
  C_k^{\text{IT}}
  =
  -\sqrt{\delta_k}\,b_k^\top z_k
  -\frac12\delta_k\Bigl[z_k^\top H_k z_k - \operatorname{tr}(H_k)\Bigr]
  -\frac12\delta_k\,\|b_k\|^2.
\end{equation}
Cost: Hessian-vector products and a trace estimate (in high dimensions,
Hutchinson-type estimation is required for $\operatorname{tr}(H_k)$).

\subsection{Corrected midpoint and trapezoidal rules}

Midpoint and trapezoidal stochastic sums approximate a Stratonovich integral.
The conversion to an It\^o integral for diffusion coefficient $g(\tau)I$ is
\[
\int g\,b\cdot dW^{\bbQ}
=
\int g\,b\circ dW^{\bbQ}
-\frac12\int g^2\,\nabla\!\cdot\! b\; d\tau,
\]
where $\nabla\!\cdot\! b = \Delta_x\log\tilde p(y\mid x)$.
A trapezoidal rule with the correction becomes
\[
C_k^{\text{trap}}
\approx
-\frac12(b_k+b_{k-1})^\top\Delta B_k
+\frac12\delta_k\,\nabla\!\cdot\! b_k
-\frac12\delta_k\,\|b_k\|^2.
\]
A simpler hybrid keeps the left-endpoint rule for the stochastic term and
applies a higher-order quadrature only to the ordinary time integral
$-\frac12\int g^2\|b\|^2 d\tau$, e.g.\ using the predictor point:
\[
C_k^{\text{hybrid}}
=
-\sqrt{\delta_k}\,b_k^\top z_k
-\frac14\delta_k\bigl(\|b_k\|^2+\|b_{\text{pred}}\|^2\bigr).
\]
This improves part of the correction while avoiding the It\^o consistency
issue in the stochastic term.

\subsection{Approximate discrete kernel ratio}

For a non-Gaussian proposal $q$, the transition is a deterministic function
$x_{k-1} = F(x_k, z_k)$.
If $F$ is invertible in $z_k$,
\[
q(x_{k-1}\mid x_k)
=
\phi(z_k)
\Bigl|\det\frac{\partial x_{k-1}}{\partial z_k}\Bigr|^{-1},
\]
where $\phi$ is the standard Gaussian density.
A corresponding evaluation for the unguided kernel $p(x_{k-1}\mid x_k)$ gives
the discrete kernel ratio directly, targeting the finite-step proposal more
accurately than a continuous-time Girsanov approximation.
Cost: inverse maps and log-determinant computations, usually prohibitive in
high dimensions; approximate log-determinants can be estimated with trace methods.

\subsection{Heuristic predictor-point correction}

Replacing $b_k$ with $b_{\text{pred}}$ in~\eqref{eq:girsanov},
\[
C_k^{\text{pred}}
=
-\sqrt{\delta_k}\,b_{\text{pred}}^\top z_k
-\tfrac12\delta_k\,\|b_{\text{pred}}\|^2,
\]
is sometimes used as an empirical heuristic.
Because $x_{\text{pred}}$ depends on the same $z_k$, this is not an
It\^o-consistent discretisation without additional correction terms.
Its performance can be tested experimentally but should not be claimed as an
automatic higher-order Girsanov rule.
